# Client acquisition system

A repeatable, low-volume system for finding small businesses that have a real
website problem, researching them honestly, and starting a conversation.

**Principles**

1. **Evidence over volume.** Ten researched messages beat 500 scraped ones.
2. **Public information only.** If the business does not publish it, do not use
   it. No logins, no paywalls, no scraping behind restrictions.
3. **One business, one email.** No mass sending, no purchased lists.
4. **Approval before contact.** Studio prepares drafts; you decide what is sent.
5. **No invented problems, results or testimonials.** Ever.

---

## 1. Choose a market

Primary: United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia. Secondary: New
Zealand, Ireland, Germany, Netherlands, UAE, Singapore.

Pick **one country and two categories per week**. Language, cultural references
and time zones make outreach better when you narrow the focus.

| Category | Why it fits | Typical hook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants & cafés | Hours/menu change often; many rely on social pages | No site, PDF menu, missing hours |
| Gyms & fitness studios | Class schedules and sign-ups | Static timetable, no mobile layout |
| Real estate agencies | Listings need structure and speed | Dated design, slow images |
| Salons & spas | Bookings and price lists | No online booking path |
| Consultants & coaches | Personal brand and credibility | One-page placeholder site |
| Local services (trades, clinics, cleaners) | High-intent local searches | No site or an unreadable one |
| Small businesses & startups | Growing, budget-aware | Unclear messaging, no CTA |

Avoid regulated or high-risk niches at the start (medical advice, finance,
adult, gambling) and anything you cannot describe accurately.

---

## 2. Find candidates

Use sources the business itself publishes:

- The official website (check it on a real phone, not just desktop).
- Google Business Profile / Bing Places listing — hours, photos, website link.
- The business's own social pages (links in bio often reveal the real site).
- Local chambers of commerce, trade associations, market directories.
- Community lists (“support local”) that include a URL.

Record where you found every field. “Looked at their Instagram bio on
2026-11-19” is evidence; “found online” is not.

**Do not**

- Bypass logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs or `robots.txt` restrictions.
- Harvest personal profiles or private contact data.
- Guess email addresses from name patterns.
- Scrape entire directories or run crawlers at scale.

**Email discovery**

Only use an address the business publishes: footer, contact page, Google
Business Profile, or a public directory listing. If there is no public email,
you can note “contact form only” and decide later — do not invent one.

---

## 3. Qualify the opportunity

Score each candidate 0–5. Work only on 3+.

| Signal | Points |
| --- | --- |
| No official website (social page only) | +2 |
| Website not usable on a phone (overflow, tiny text, pinching) | +2 |
| Clearly outdated (stale copyright, broken layout, Flash-era design) | +1 |
| Missing basics: hours, location, contact, clear services | +1 |
| Slow or insecure (`http://`, huge images, 10s+ load) | +1 |
| No clear call to action or enquiry path | +1 |
| Currently running paid ads or promotions | +1 |
| Business is active (recent posts/reviews, open now) | +1 |

Disqualify if:

- The site was redesigned recently and works well.
- The business is a franchise with a mandated corporate site.
- You cannot find a legitimate public contact route.
- The business is closed, for sale, or clearly inactive.
- The category requires expertise or compliance you cannot support.

Write the **specific observation** while you are looking at the site. One
sentence, concrete, verifiable:

> “On a 390px phone the menu opens as a 6-page PDF and the opening hours only
> appear in a footer image.”

Not:

> “Your website is bad and outdated.”

---

## 4. Record the lead

Add it in Studio → Leads, or use
[leads/leads.csv](../leads/leads.csv) as a worksheet. Minimum fields:

- Business name, country, category
- Official website (or “none”)
- Public email and phone (only if published)
- Observed problems, suggested improvements, opportunity
- Source URL and date verified
- Status and follow-up date

For each entry, add a source URL in your notes. If a claim cannot be traced back
to a public page, remove it.

**Never store** passwords, OTPs, banking data, ID documents, health data or
anything a client has not chosen to publish.

---

## 5. Prepare outreach

Draft in Studio → Outreach. Choose the template that matches the actual
situation:

| Situation | Template |
| --- | --- |
| No website at all | No website yet |
| Site is old or broken on mobile | Outdated website |
| Site works but looks behind the brand | Redesign opportunity |
| Ads/promotions with no focused page | Landing page offer |
| No reply after 4–7 days | Follow-up (once) |

Replace every bracketed placeholder with something only you could have noticed.
Read it aloud. If it could be sent to any other business unchanged, rewrite it.

See [outreach-guide.md](outreach-guide.md) for the full sequence and legal
notes.

---

## 6. Weekly rhythm

A sustainable solo routine:

| Day | Task | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Pick country + 2 categories; build a list of 10 candidates | 60 min |
| Tue | Verify 5 candidates on mobile; write observations | 60 min |
| Wed | Draft 5 personalized emails; review and send 3–5 | 45 min |
| Thu | Follow up on last week's non-replies (one follow-up only) | 20 min |
| Fri | Update statuses, log drafts, export a JSON backup | 20 min |
| Sun | Review replies; prepare proposals for interested leads | 30 min |

Track only what you act on: leads added, emails sent, replies, conversations,
proposals, projects won. Do not invent conversion rates; measure your own after
20–30 sends.

---

## 7. What realistic progress looks like

Cold outreach to small businesses is slow. A realistic early benchmark is a
small single-digit reply rate from genuinely personalized messages, with most
replies arriving after a follow-up. Results vary by market, offer and timing.

Do not promise yourself or a client a number. Improve the inputs:

- A more specific observation.
- A smaller, better-matched niche.
- A clearer, lower-risk first step (a concept, an audit, a single page).
- Faster, more human replies when someone does respond.

---

## 8. When someone says yes

1. Move the lead to **Replied** or **Interested**.
2. Ask three questions: what must the site achieve, who is it for, what is the
   deadline.
3. Generate a proposal in Studio and edit it to match the conversation.
4. Send it yourself; move the lead to **Proposal sent**.
5. Create a project only when the scope is agreed. Do not mark it paid or
   completed without verifying the facts.

This system exists to keep the work honest and traceable — not to automate
spam.
