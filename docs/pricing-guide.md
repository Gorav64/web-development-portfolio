# Pricing guide

Beginner rates, set by Vishal, in Indian rupees. They are deliberately low to win
first projects and build a portfolio. They are **target prices, not fixed prices**:
every project gets its own requirement review and a suggested figure inside the
tier. No price is quoted or finalised without Vishal's explicit approval.

---

## The three tiers

| Service | INR | USD approx. | Typical build effort |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Basic landing page | ₹3,000 | $32 | 3–5 working days |
| Small business website | ₹4,000 | $43 | 5–7 working days |
| Simple website redesign | ₹3,000–₹4,000 | $32–$43 | 4–6 working days |

USD figures use a **₹93 per USD planning rate** (20 November 2026). Rates move;
recheck before quoting and state the rate and date on anything you send. Effort
covers build time only, not waiting for client content, feedback or approvals.

---

## What the ₹70,000–₹80,000 target means

It is a **savings goal for a better laptop, not a per-website price.** At ₹3,000–₹4,000
per project, reaching it takes roughly **18–27 paid projects** before fees and taxes.
For reference, the 20 researched leads in `leads/` would total ₹75,000–₹78,000 only
if every single one converted. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.

Do not inflate a quote to hit the target faster, and do not promise a client that a
website will produce a set number of enquiries or sales.

---

## Work out a price in five steps

1. **Collect the requirement in writing.** Pages, sections, content, images, forms,
   booking, menu, payments, languages, deadline, and who supplies each asset.
2. **Match it to a tier.** One page and a single call to action is a landing page.
   A short multi-page site is a small business website. Existing-site surgery is a
   redesign.
3. **Estimate the effort.** Count pages and features, note missing content, then
   compare against the typical build effort above. If the job is clearly larger than
   the tier, say so and propose a scope that fits — do not silently absorb extra work.
4. **Add only the extras that were agreed.** Use the add-on table below as a starting
   point, then adjust for the actual work. Show each extra as a separate line so the
   client can decline it.
5. **Present INR and USD together**, list what is included and what is not, and mark
   it as an estimate subject to approval. Send nothing until Vishal approves.

## What is included

### Basic landing page — ₹3,000

- One responsive page (mobile, tablet, desktop)
- Hero, services or features, proof or gallery area, contact call to action
- Working navigation, buttons and one contact form if needed
- Basic on-page SEO: page title, meta description, heading structure
- Source files and a short handover note
- Two rounds of small revisions

### Small business website — ₹4,000

- Up to four responsive pages (for example home, about, services, contact)
- Shared header, navigation and footer; clear calls to action on every page
- Contact form, phone and email links, map embed and opening hours where relevant
- Basic on-page SEO on each page
- Cross-browser and phone-size checks
- Source files and a short handover note
- Two rounds of small revisions

### Simple website redesign — ₹3,000–₹4,000

- Review of the current site and its content
- New layout and visual direction applied to up to four existing pages
- Responsive rebuild, working navigation, forms and calls to action
- Content carried over; light polishing only, not full copywriting
- Basic on-page SEO on the rebuilt pages
- Source files and a short handover note
- Two rounds of small revisions

The exact figure inside the redesign range depends on page count, how much content
must be rewritten, and whether the existing site can supply usable text and images.

## Costs that are not in the price

Name these in every quote:

- **Domain** — roughly ₹1,000–₹1,500 per year for a `.com`; other endings vary. Bought
  in the client's name.
- **Hosting** — a static site can run free on Netlify, Vercel or GitHub Pages. Paid
  shared hosting is often ₹150–₹500 per month. Bought in the client's name.
- **Business email** — free tiers exist; paid plans are typically ₹100–₹300 per user
  per month.
- **Paid assets** — stock photos, premium fonts, plugins, booking or CRM subscriptions.
- **Payments and transfer fees** — on a ₹4,000 international project, fixed and
  percentage fees can take a noticeable share. Agree who pays before starting.
- **Taxes** — the client's and Vishal's local rules apply. Do not give tax advice.
- **Ongoing maintenance, new features and emergency support** — separate agreement.

These ranges are general guidance, not quotes. Check the real price at the registrar,
host or payment provider before telling a client a number.

## International clients

- Show the figure in **INR and USD**. If the client asks for GBP, AUD or CAD, convert
  at the current rate on the day and label it as indicative.
- Agree the invoice currency and payment method before work starts. The amount that
  actually arrives can differ from the quoted figure because of fees and conversion.
- Never ask for card details, banking passwords or one-time codes by email, and never
  handle a client's bank account.
- Quote in English, use plain wording, and avoid slang or abbreviations.

---

## Milestones

- Under ₹25,000: **50% deposit / 50% on delivery**
- ₹25,000 and above: **40% deposit / 40% at the design-and-build milestone /
  20% on delivery**

These are starting structures. Never mark a payment as received until it is visible
in your own account.

---

## Revisions

- Two rounds included, one consolidated list per round.
- Extra rounds: suggested ₹500 each.
- A round covers small changes to an approved direction. It does not cover a new page,
  a new concept or a change of scope.

---

## Quote responsibly

1. Start from the scope, not the number. The tier is a floor for a clear, small job.
2. If key details are missing, give a range and say what changes it.
3. State clearly that it is an estimate, confirmed after a requirements review.
4. Never promise rankings, traffic, bookings, revenue or "10× conversions".
5. Do not underprice further to win. A project you cannot finish well costs more than
   a declined quote.
6. Put the scope, price, inclusions and exclusions in writing.
7. Get Vishal's explicit approval before any quote or email goes out.

## When to change these numbers

Review after every three completed projects. If you win every enquiry instantly, the
price is probably too low. Raise it gradually and keep the old rate for anyone already
in conversation. Update:

- `studio/js/data.js` — project bases, add-ons and the USD rate
- `tools/build_lead_system.py` — `RATE_INR_PER_USD` and the approved tiers
- this guide — the table, efforts and add-on suggestions

Then rerun `py tools/build_lead_system.py` so the CSV, reports and dashboard match.
