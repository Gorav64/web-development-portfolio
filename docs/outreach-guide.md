# Outreach guide

Cold email only works when it is specific, short and honest. This guide covers
the sequence, the five templates used in Studio, and the rules that keep you
out of trouble.

**Studio never sends email.** It creates editable drafts. You review them in
your own email client and press send yourself, one business at a time, after
your own approval.

---

## The rules

1. One email per business, written for that business.
2. Replace every `[bracketed]` placeholder with a real observation.
3. No attachments, no tracking pixels, no fake urgency on the first email.
4. One follow-up only, 4–7 days later. If there is no reply, stop.
5. Respect opt-outs immediately and note “do not contact” on the lead.
6. Never claim results, rankings, revenue or testimonials you cannot prove.
7. Identify yourself truthfully and use a real reply-to address.
8. Check the rules for the recipient's country before sending (see below).

---

## Message structure

A good first email has four beats:

1. **Observation** — something true and specific about *their* business.
2. **Consequence** — why it matters to a customer, in one sentence.
3. **Offer** — a small, low-risk next step.
4. **Question** — easy to answer, no pressure.

Keep it under 150 words. Plain text. No images, no long signature, no calendar
link on the first contact.

Subject lines: 3–7 words, specific, no clickbait, no fake “Re:”.

- A website idea for [Business]
- A quick note about [Business]'s website
- [Business] — mobile menu question
- Quick idea for [Business]

## The sequence

| Day | Action | Template |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | First email | Choose one of 1–4 based on the real situation |
| 4–7 | Single follow-up, only if no reply | Template 5 |
| After that | Stop. Mark “Not a fit” or leave in Contacted with a note | — |

If someone replies “not now”, ask whether you may check back in a few months
and record the date. If they say no, mark the lead **Not a fit** and do not
contact them again.

## Template 1 — No website yet

Use when there is no official site, or only a social page.

**Subject:** A website idea for [Business]

```text
Hi [FirstName],

I came across [Business] while looking at [category] businesses in [country] —
[one genuine, specific observation, e.g. "your Instagram shows a new menu every
week but there's no single place with your hours and location"].

I noticed there doesn't seem to be a website for [Business] yet. A simple, fast
site would give people one reliable place to see what you offer, your hours and
how to get in touch.

I'm a web developer and I build clean, mobile-friendly websites for small
businesses. If it's useful, I can put together a short, no-obligation homepage
concept so you can see what it could look like before deciding anything.

Would that be worth a look?

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Portfolio URL]
[Your email]
```

## Template 2 — Outdated website

Use when a site exists but is dated, broken on mobile, or missing basics.

**Subject:** A quick note about [Business]'s website

```text
Hi [FirstName],

I had a look at [website] and noticed [specific issue, e.g. "the menu opens as a
PDF that's hard to read on a phone"].

For customers finding you on a phone, that can make it harder to check what you
offer and get in touch. A focused refresh — rather than a full rebuild — could
fix the parts that matter most without changing the brand you've built.

I'm an independent web developer working on exactly this kind of modernization:
same content, cleaner structure, faster on mobile.

I can send a short list of specific fixes with no obligation. Would that be
helpful?

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Portfolio URL]
[Your email]
```

## Template 3 — Redesign opportunity

Use when the site works but undersells the business.

**Subject:** [Business] — a faster, mobile-first website

```text
Hi [FirstName],

I was looking at [Business] online and thought the business comes across better
than the website does. [One concrete design or UX issue you observed.]

I redesign small-business websites with a focus on three things: clear
structure, a strong mobile experience and fast loading. [The specific
improvement you would make first.]

I could prepare a redesigned homepage concept for [Business] — one page, your
real content — so you can compare it side by side with what you have now.

If you'd like to see it, just reply and I'll get started.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Portfolio URL]
[Your email]
```

## Template 4 — Landing page offer

Use when the business promotes an offer, event or service but has no focused
page.

**Subject:** A landing page idea for [Business]

```text
Hi [FirstName],

I noticed [Business] is active on social media, but there's no single page that
explains your offer and captures enquiries in one place.

A focused landing page — headline, offer, proof, hours/location and one call to
action — is often the fastest way to turn interest into bookings or calls.
[Suggest the specific offer or angle you would build the page around.]

I build lightweight landing pages in HTML, CSS and JavaScript: fast, responsive
and easy to update. I can prepare a concept for [Business] if you want to see
the direction first.

Would you like me to send a quick draft?

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Portfolio URL]
[Your email]
```

## Template 5 — Follow-up (send once)

Use 4–7 days after the first email, only if there was no reply.

**Subject:** Following up — [Business]

```text
Hi [FirstName],

Just following up on my note about [Business]'s website. I know inboxes get busy,
so no pressure at all.

If a quick homepage concept would still be useful, I'm happy to prepare one — no
obligation and nothing to sign. If the timing isn't right, just let me know and
I'll close the loop.

Either way, wishing [Business] a good week.

Best regards,
[Your name]
[Portfolio URL]
[Your email]
```

## Handling replies

| Reply | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| “Tell me more” / “How much?” | Ask two context questions, then send a short range and offer a 15-minute call. Log status **Interested**. |
| “Not now” | Ask if you may follow up in 3–6 months; set a follow-up date; do not keep emailing. |
| “No” / “Unsubscribe” / “Stop” | Mark **Not a fit**, add a do-not-contact note, do not reply again. |
| Question about the site | Answer it directly; link to the specific page or screenshot. |
| Angry / accusatory | Apologize briefly, confirm you will not contact them again, stop. |

When you send a proposal, update the lead to **Proposal sent** and create a
project only once the scope is agreed.

## Legal and deliverability notes

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules differ by country and change
over time.*

- **Identify yourself.** Use your real name, a working reply address and an
  honest subject line.
- **Include a postal address if your jurisdiction requires one.** CAN-SPAM
  (US) requires a valid physical postal address in commercial email. Check what
  applies to you before sending. Do not use an address you have no permission
  to publish.
- **Opt-out must work.** Honour any request to stop immediately and keep a
  record of it.
- **GDPR / PECR (UK & EU).** B2B cold email rules vary by country and by whether
  the recipient is a sole trader or a company. Legitimate interest may apply,
  but you must be able to explain it, keep data minimal and delete it on
  request.
- **CASL (Canada)** generally requires consent (express or implied) and clear
  identification. Implied consent may exist for publicly listed business
  addresses, but it is time-limited.
- **Australia (Spam Act)** requires consent and a functional unsubscribe.
- **Gmail / Outlook limits.** Low-volume, personalized, plain-text mail from a
  real account is far safer than bursts from a new address. Do not use bulk
  sending tools.

If a market's rules are unclear, ask before sending — or choose a market you
can research confidently.

## Quality check before pressing send

- [ ] The observation is true and specific to this business.
- [ ] The email is under 150 words and reads like a person wrote it.
- [ ] No promises about traffic, rankings, sales or timelines.
- [ ] No fake testimonial, logo or client claim.
- [ ] The offer is a small next step, not a contract.
- [ ] The recipient's address is publicly published.
- [ ] You have not contacted them before.
- [ ] Your identification and opt-out path are clear.
- [ ] The lead exists in Studio with a source note.
